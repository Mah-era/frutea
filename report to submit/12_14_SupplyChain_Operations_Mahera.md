# 12. Supply Chain Management Plan

> **Author:** Mahera · **Appendix section:** D · **Report parts:** 12, 14
> **Product context:** FruTea is an **instant fruit-tea powder** — a spray-dried blend of tea extract, fruit powder, and food-grade flavouring that dissolves instantly in hot or cold water. The Year 1 consumer range is sold as 15 g single sachets at BDT 20 and a ten-sachet Mix Pack at BDT 200 across five flavours (Lemon, Mango, Lychee, Orange, Strawberry). A 12 g Low-Sugar Green Tea sachet is planned for Year 2, after Year 1 is complete, at BDT 35.
> **Manufacturing model:** *Hybrid* — Year 1 uses a contract manufacturer (co-packer) for blending and sachet filling; FruTea transitions to its own production facility in Years 2–3 as volume grows.

---

## 12.1 Supply Chain Management Objectives

FruTea's supply chain is designed around a low-capital, asset-light launch that can scale predictably. The objectives are:

1. **Continuous availability** — maintain a service level of **≥ 95%** on fast-moving SKUs (Lemon and Mango single sachets) so that shelves and canteens are never empty during peak demand.
2. **Cost efficiency** — keep total landed supply-chain cost (sourcing + inbound + storage + outbound) below **18% of the ex-factory selling price** through shared co-packing capacity and a centrally located rented warehouse.
3. **Quality and food safety** — ensure every batch is traceable and compliant with **BSTI** standards and **BFSA (Bangladesh Food Safety Authority)** hygiene requirements, from raw-material intake to final dispatch.
4. **Flexibility and scalability** — build a supply chain that can absorb a **3× volume increase** over three years without structural redesign, simply by adding co-packing shifts and, later, in-house lines.
5. **Supplier reliability** — dual-source all critical raw materials so that no single supplier failure can halt production.
6. **Freshness and shelf life** — move finished goods from production to retail within a target **7–10 day cycle** to preserve the product's stated 12-month shelf life.

---

## 12.2 Supply Chain Overview

FruTea operates a **make-to-stock, hub-and-spoke supply chain**. The five stages are:

**Suppliers → Contract Manufacturer (co-packer) → FruTea Central Warehouse (Dhaka) → Distributors / Channel partners → End customers.**

- **Upstream (inbound):** Raw materials (tea extract, fruit powder, sweetener, acidulants, additives) and packaging materials are procured from local and imported suppliers and delivered to the co-packer.
- **Midstream (conversion):** The co-packer blends, fills, seals, labels, and cartons the product to FruTea's specification and quality standards.
- **Downstream (outbound):** Finished goods are consolidated at FruTea's rented central warehouse in Dhaka, then distributed to retail, super-shop, university/canteen, café, corporate, and e-commerce/f-commerce channels.

## 12.3 Raw Material Sourcing

Because FruTea is an **instant (spray-dried) fruit-tea powder** and not a brewed loose-tea or tea-bag product, its bill of materials mirrors that of comparable instant iced-tea powders (e.g., Nestea, Lipton Ice Tea powder, Tang-style beverage powders). The formulation is built from a functional tea base, a sweetening system, an acid/flavour system, and stabilising additives, all dry-blended and filled into moisture-barrier sachets.

### 12.3.1 Core raw materials (the powder blend)

| # | Raw material | Function in FruTea | Typical share of blend | Preferred sourcing |
|---|--------------|--------------------|------------------------|--------------------|
| 1 | **Instant tea extract (spray-dried tea solids)** | The tea base that gives colour, aroma, and the natural caffeine "tea" character; instantly soluble | 8–15% | Local processors (Sylhet/Sreemangal instant-tea units) + import as backup |
| 2 | **Sugar (fine sucrose / dextrose)** | Primary sweetener and bulking agent; carries flavour | 55–70% | Local (Bangladesh sugar mills / commercial refiners) |
| 3 | **Maltodextrin** | Carrier and free-flow bulking agent; improves solubility and mouthfeel | 5–10% | Import (India/China) |
| 4 | **Citric acid (± malic acid)** | Acidulant delivering the tangy, "fruity-tea" refreshment note | 2–4% | Import (China/India), locally available through chemical importers |
| 5 | **Fruit powder / spray-dried fruit juice** (mango, lemon, lychee, orange, strawberry) | Real-fruit character and natural taste identity per flavour | 2–5% | Import (India/Thailand/China) — limited local availability |
| 6 | **Nature-identical / natural flavouring** | Reinforces and stabilises the fruit note; ensures consistency | 0.5–2% | Import from certified food-flavour houses |
| 7 | **Food colour (natural: beta-carotene, anthocyanin; or approved synthetic)** | Visual appeal matching each flavour | < 0.5% | Import (food-grade certified) |
| 8 | **Ascorbic acid (Vitamin C)** | Antioxidant + light nutritional claim | < 0.5% | Import |
| 9 | **Anti-caking / free-flow agent (silicon dioxide, tricalcium phosphate)** | Prevents clumping; keeps powder pourable in humid conditions | < 1% | Import |
| 10 | **Sweetener for Low-Sugar line (sucralose / stevia)** | Enables the reduced-sugar Green Tea variant | replaces sugar | Import (certified) |

### 12.3.2 Packaging materials

| # | Material | Function | Sourcing |
|---|----------|----------|----------|
| 1 | **Laminated sachet film** (PET / metallised / PE multilayer) | Primary moisture & light barrier — critical for a hygroscopic powder | Local flexible-packaging converters (Dhaka/Gazipur) |
| 2 | **Box/carton (mono-carton)** for mix & box packs | Secondary retail packaging + branding surface | Local printing/packaging suppliers |
| 4 | **Shipping cartons + tape + stretch film** | Tertiary packaging for transport | Local |
| 5 | **Labels / batch-code stickers** | Traceability, MRP, expiry | Local |

### 12.3.3 Sourcing rationale (mix of local + import)

FruTea follows a **balanced local-plus-import sourcing strategy**, which is both realistic for a Bangladeshi startup and defensible academically:

- **Sourced locally:** sugar, instant tea solids (from the Sylhet tea belt), all packaging materials, and cartons. This shortens lead times, lowers freight and duty, supports the report's **local-sourcing sustainability claim**, and creates domestic employment.
- **Imported (specialised inputs):** fruit powders, flavour concentrates, food colours, acidulants, maltodextrin, and specialty sweeteners — these are not reliably produced at food-grade scale in Bangladesh and must be procured from certified international suppliers through licensed importers.

This split keeps the majority of raw-material **weight and cost local** (sugar dominates the blend) while importing only the low-volume, high-value functional ingredients — minimising exposure to import duty, LC delays, and forex risk.

## 12.4 Supplier Selection Criteria

Suppliers are evaluated and shortlisted using a **weighted scoring model**. Each candidate supplier is scored 1–5 on the following criteria; the weighted total determines approval.

| Criterion | Weight | Why it matters for FruTea |
|-----------|--------|---------------------------|
| **Product quality & food-grade certification** (ISO 22000 / HACCP / BSTI) | 25% | Non-negotiable for an ingestible product |
| **Price competitiveness** | 20% | Protects thin startup margins |
| **Reliability & on-time delivery** | 20% | Prevents stock-outs and line stoppages |
| **Lead time & flexibility** | 15% | Supports demand swings and small early orders |
| **Minimum order quantity (MOQ)** | 10% | Low MOQ is critical in the low-volume launch phase |
| **Financial stability & reputation** | 5% | Ensures continuity of supply |
| **Sustainability / ethical practices** | 5% | Aligns with brand values |

**Sourcing policy:** every **critical** raw material (tea extract, fruit powder, flavour, sachet film) must have **at least two approved suppliers** (dual sourcing) to eliminate single-point-of-failure risk. Non-critical commodities (cartons, tape) may be single-sourced.

## 12.5 Inbound Logistics

Inbound logistics governs the movement of raw and packaging materials **into the co-packer**.

- **Local materials** (sugar, tea solids, packaging film, cartons) are delivered by supplier trucks or FruTea-arranged covered trucks directly to the co-packing facility on a scheduled basis.
- **Imported materials** arrive by sea at **Chattogram Port**, clear customs through a C&F (clearing & forwarding) agent, and are trucked (~250 km) to Dhaka. Because imports carry a long lead time (typically **6–10 weeks** door-to-door including LC and clearance), they are ordered against forecast, not against live demand.
- **Goods-inward inspection:** every consignment is checked for quantity, documentation, certificate of analysis (CoA), and physical/quality parameters before acceptance. Rejected lots are quarantined and returned.
- **Consolidation:** to minimise freight cost, local raw materials are ordered in **full-truckload (FTL)** quantities where storage allows, and imported specialty inputs are consolidated into fewer, larger LC shipments.

## 12.6 Inventory Management

FruTea holds three inventory types: **raw materials**, **packaging**, and **finished goods**. Given the 12-month shelf life and hygroscopic nature of the powder, inventory is managed on a **FEFO (First-Expiry-First-Out)** basis.

**Policy parameters (illustrative Year-1 assumptions):**

- **Safety stock** is held to cover demand variability during replenishment lead time. Because imported ingredients have long, variable lead times, they carry a **higher safety stock (6–8 weeks)** than locally sourced sugar/packaging (**1–2 weeks**).
- **Reorder Point (ROP)** = (Average daily usage × Lead time in days) + Safety stock.
- **Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)** is used for high-value imported inputs to balance ordering cost against holding cost.
- **ABC classification:** tea extract, fruit powder, and flavour are **"A" items** (tight control, frequent review); sugar and film are **"B" items**; cartons, tape, labels are **"C" items** (bulk-ordered, loose control).
- **Finished-goods buffer:** the central warehouse holds roughly **2–3 weeks** of finished-goods cover on fast-movers to guarantee the 95% service level.

## 12.7 Production Planning

Production is **make-to-stock**, driven by a rolling demand forecast and a **Master Production Schedule (MPS)**.

- **Year 1 (contract manufacturing):** FruTea books co-packing capacity in **weekly production runs**. Each run batches by flavour to minimise changeover/cleaning downtime, prioritising Lemon and Mango (the highest-demand SKUs). Production quantity per SKU = forecast demand + target finished-goods buffer − current stock.
- **Years 2–3 (own production):** FruTea installs its own blending and sachet-filling lines and moves to a continuous internal schedule, retaining the co-packer only as overflow/surge capacity.
- **Batch sizing** balances setup cost against holding cost; changeovers between flavours are grouped to reduce cleaning cycles and cross-contamination risk.

---

## 12.8 Warehousing

FruTea operates from a **single rented central warehouse in Dhaka** during the launch phase (location justification and cost math in **Section 14.1**). Warehouse design principles:

- **Zoned layout:** (1) inbound/receiving & QC hold, (2) ambient dry storage for finished goods on pallet racking, (3) picking/packing/dispatch, (4) returns & quarantine.
- **Storage conditions:** cool, dry, low-humidity ambient storage — essential because instant tea powder is hygroscopic; no cold chain is required, which keeps warehousing cost low.
- **FEFO picking** enforced through batch/expiry labelling.
- **Right-sizing:** a compact **~1,500–2,500 sq ft** rented unit is sufficient for Year-1 throughput, avoiding over-investment in space.

## 12.9 Outbound Distribution

Finished goods move from the central warehouse to customers through a **tiered distribution network**:

- **Distributor-led (primary):** FruTea sells to appointed regional distributors, who break bulk and service traditional retail, tea stalls, and small shops.
- **Direct-to-channel (secondary):** for **super shops** (Shwapno, Meena Bazar, Agora), **universities/canteens**, **cafés/restaurants**, and **corporate/office** accounts, FruTea delivers directly or via key-account logistics.
- **E-commerce / F-commerce:** online orders are fulfilled from the same warehouse via third-party courier/parcel services (Pathao, RedX, Sundarban, etc.).

**Transport mode:** intra-Dhaka deliveries use small covered vans/pickups; inter-city shipments to regional distributors use shared/contracted covered trucks. FruTea **outsources transportation (3PL/contract carriers)** rather than owning a fleet, converting a fixed cost into a variable per-trip cost during the capital-light launch.

## 12.10 Demand Forecasting

Because FruTea is a new brand with no sales history, forecasting starts **qualitative** and becomes **quantitative** as data accumulates:

- **Launch phase:** forecasts are built bottom-up from planned channel coverage (number of retail outlets × expected reorder rate), primary market research (surveys, taste tests), and analogues from comparable instant-beverage powders.
- **Post-launch:** FruTea adopts **time-series forecasting (moving average / exponential smoothing)** on actual sell-through data, adjusted for **seasonality** — demand for the cold-drink use-case peaks in the hot months (March–September), so production is built ahead of summer.
- **Forecast accuracy** is tracked via **MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error)**; the target is to reach **≤ 20% MAPE** by end of Year 1.
- Forecasts feed directly into the MPS and inventory reorder logic (a **pull-informed push** system).

## 12.11 Quality Control in Supply Chain

Quality is controlled at **every node**, not just at manufacturing:

| Stage | Quality checkpoint |
|-------|--------------------|
| **Supplier** | Certificate of Analysis (CoA), food-grade certification, pre-shipment sample approval |
| **Goods inward** | Incoming inspection — identity, weight, moisture, foreign matter, documentation |
| **In-process (co-packer)** | Blend uniformity, correct dosing, sachet seal integrity, fill weight, batch coding |
| **Finished goods** | Solubility test, taste/aroma panel, moisture content, packaging leak test, expiry coding |
| **Warehouse** | Storage-condition monitoring (humidity), FEFO compliance, damage checks |
| **Outbound** | Dispatch inspection, correct SKU/quantity, undamaged cartons |

All batches are **traceable via batch codes**, enabling targeted recall if any lot fails — an essential food-safety safeguard. Standards followed: **BSTI, BFSA, HACCP principles.**

## 12.12 SCM Risks and Mitigation

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|--------|------------|
| **Import delay / port congestion** (fruit powder, flavour) | Medium | High | Dual sourcing + 6–8 week safety stock on imported inputs; order against forecast |
| **Forex fluctuation / import-cost rise** | High | Medium | Localise wherever possible; forward-plan LCs; build cost buffer into pricing |
| **Raw-material price volatility** (sugar) | Medium | Medium | Contracted supply agreements; multiple local suppliers |
| **Supplier quality failure** | Low | High | CoA + incoming QC + approved-supplier list + quarantine |
| **Co-packer capacity / reliability** (Year 1 dependency) | Medium | High | Backup co-packer identified; hybrid plan to bring production in-house |
| **Moisture damage / clumping in storage/transit** | Medium | Medium | Barrier film + anti-caking agent + humidity-controlled dry storage |
| **Demand forecast error** | High | Medium | Safety stock + short replenishment cycle + rolling forecast updates |
| **Transport disruption** (hartal, fuel, floods) | Medium | Medium | Multiple 3PL carriers; regional distributor stock buffers |

# 14. Operations Plan

## 14.1 Business Location — Rented Warehouse & Distribution Hub

FruTea rents (rather than buys) its central warehouse to stay **capital-light** during launch. The location decision is made on a **total-cost-to-serve basis**, weighing rent against inbound and outbound transportation. Three candidate locations were compared:

- **Option A — Tejgaon / central Dhaka** (inside the main market)
- **Option B — Savar / Gazipur** (Dhaka outskirts, industrial belt)
- **Option C — Sylhet** (near the tea-source belt)

### 14.1.1 Cost assumptions (illustrative, Year 1)

- **Monthly finished-goods throughput:** ~**8 tonnes** of product (≈ the Year-1 average).
- **Warehouse size required:** ~**2,000 sq ft**.
- **Market gravity:** ~**80%** of FruTea's demand (universities, super shops, cafés, offices) is concentrated **inside Dhaka**; only ~20% is regional.
- **Freight benchmarks (Bangladesh, covered truck/van):** intra-Dhaka delivery ≈ **৳2,000/trip**; Dhaka outskirts → Dhaka ≈ **৳4,000/trip**; Sylhet ↔ Dhaka (~240 km) ≈ **৳15,000/trip**.
- **Rent benchmarks:** central Dhaka ≈ **৳50/sq ft/mo**; Dhaka outskirts ≈ **৳25/sq ft/mo**; Sylhet ≈ **৳18/sq ft/mo**.

### 14.1.2 Monthly cost comparison

| Cost component | A. Central Dhaka | B. Savar/Gazipur | C. Sylhet |
|----------------|------------------|------------------|-----------|
| **Warehouse rent** (2,000 sq ft) | 2,000 × ৳50 = **৳100,000** | 2,000 × ৳25 = **৳50,000** | 2,000 × ৳18 = **৳36,000** |
| **Inbound freight** (co-packer/port → warehouse) | Low (co-packer near Dhaka) ≈ **৳20,000** | Low ≈ **৳25,000** | High (import + local inputs hauled to Sylhet) ≈ **৳70,000** |
| **Outbound freight** (warehouse → ~80% Dhaka market) | Low, short intra-city trips ≈ **৳40,000** | Medium (edge-of-city runs) ≈ **৳70,000** | Very high (haul finished goods back to Dhaka market) ≈ **৳160,000** |
| **Total monthly logistics + rent** | **≈ ৳160,000** | **≈ ৳145,000** | **≈ ৳266,000** |

### 14.1.3 Decision

Although central Dhaka has the **highest rent**, its **low outbound freight** (because it sits inside the demand centre) makes its **total cost-to-serve** nearly identical to the cheaper-rent outskirts option — while delivering **faster service, shorter lead times, and a higher fill rate**. Sylhet, despite the cheapest rent, is decisively rejected: locating away from the market forces finished goods to be hauled **back into Dhaka**, roughly **65% more expensive** overall.

> **Chosen location: a rented ~2,000 sq ft warehouse in the Tejgaon/central-Dhaka industrial area** — the point that minimises total distribution cost *and* maximises responsiveness to the 80% of demand located in Dhaka. As volume grows, a second regional stocking point can be added later without moving the hub.

## 14.2 Daily Operational Activities

A typical operating day at the FruTea warehouse/hub follows a fixed rhythm:

| Time block | Activity |
|-----------|----------|
| **Morning** | Open facility; review overnight online/B2B orders; check stock levels & reorder alerts; goods-inward receiving and QC of any incoming raw/packaging or finished consignments |
| **Mid-day** | Pick, pack, and stage finished-goods orders (FEFO); coordinate distributor and courier pickups; update inventory records |
| **Afternoon** | Dispatch deliveries to channels; handle customer-service queries and B2B account calls; process returns/quarantine |
| **End of day** | Reconcile dispatches vs. orders; update sales/inventory dashboards; place replenishment orders with suppliers/co-packer; secure facility |
| **Weekly** | Place co-packer production booking; supplier ordering; KPI review; cycle count of A-items |

## 14.3 Order Processing System

FruTea uses a single **order-to-delivery workflow** across all channels:

**Order received → Order verified & stock checked → Payment/credit confirmed → Picking (FEFO) → Packing & invoicing → Dispatch → Delivery → Confirmation & record update.**

- **B2B / distributor orders** arrive via phone, WhatsApp, or sales-rep visits and are entered into the order system.
- **E-commerce / F-commerce orders** flow from the website and social channels and are fulfilled via courier.
- **Payment:** B2B on agreed credit terms; retail/online via cash-on-delivery or mobile financial services (bKash/Nagad).
- Orders inside Dhaka target **same-/next-day** fulfilment; regional orders are consolidated for scheduled dispatch.

## 14.4 Customer Service Plan

- **Channels:** phone hotline, WhatsApp Business, email, and social-media DM (Facebook/Instagram) — the platforms FruTea's young target market already uses.
- **B2B care:** dedicated key-account handling for distributors, super shops, cafés, and corporate buyers, including reorder reminders and issue resolution.
- **Complaint handling:** logged, acknowledged within **24 hours**, and resolved with replacement/refund for any quality issue, with the batch code traced back through QC.
- **Feedback loop:** customer feedback and complaints feed directly into product and quality improvement.

---

## 14.5 Technology and Record Keeping

FruTea runs a **lean, low-cost digital operations stack** appropriate to a startup:

- **Inventory & order management:** a cloud-based inventory/POS tool (or a structured spreadsheet system initially) tracking stock, batches, expiry (FEFO), and reorder points.
- **Accounting:** digital bookkeeping software for sales, purchases, and cash flow.
- **Sales & CRM:** simple CRM/spreadsheet to manage distributor and B2B accounts and reorder cycles.
- **E-commerce:** the FruTea website and F-commerce (Facebook/Instagram shop) for direct online orders.
- **Records retained:** batch/production records, supplier CoAs, QC logs, inventory ledgers, sales and dispatch records, and customer-complaint logs — all essential for **traceability, audit, and food-safety compliance.**
- **Data-driven control:** KPI dashboards (Sections 12.13 & 14.6) are updated from these systems to guide decisions.
